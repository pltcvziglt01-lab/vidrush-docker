EDITING AND RHYTHM — applies to every comedy clip. Read this before writing any
shot. A clip that only sets something up is a failed clip.

EVERY CLIP IS ITS OWN JOKE: each eight second clip must have a beginning, a
middle and a small payoff of its own. It may set up the next clip as well, but
it can never be pure setup. If a viewer watched only this one clip, they must
still get something.

NO DEAD AIR: something must happen every single second — an action, a reaction,
a sound, an effect, a change of expression. Two characters standing still doing
nothing is dead air, never comedy. Count the seconds and fill each one.

A HOLD IS ONE SECOND, NOT TWO: a pause only works when it lands right after a
fast burst and sits on ONE face that is visibly processing something. Anything
longer, or a pause on two motionless characters, is dead.

STACK SMALL GAGS: the main joke is not the only joke. Layer cheap visual gags
underneath it — paint splattered on his own face and not noticed, a handprint
left on his own belly, birds bursting out of the cave at a shout. Two or three
of these per clip cost nothing and fill every second.

DISMISSAL BEATS CONFRONTATION: a character who glances, grunts and walks
straight past is funnier than one who stops and stares. Reaction should be
under-sized, quick and mobile. Never park two characters in front of each other
to "react".

END ON A FACE, NOT A TABLEAU: close the clip on one character's expression
changing — a grin freezing, a tuft of hair drooping, eyes flicking to camera —
not on a wide shot of everyone standing there.

SHOT BUDGET — learned the hard way, 26 Aug 2026.

EIGHT SECONDS HOLDS THREE BEATS, NOT SEVEN. Cramming seven actions into eight
seconds makes the model render all of them half-finished. Density does not mean
more plot — it means filling the seconds between beats with reactions, small
expression changes and sound. Three beats, richly played, always beats seven
beats rushed.

A CHARACTER WHO ENTERS MUST NOT BE IN THE START FRAME. The start frame is where
everyone already is. If someone has to walk in, walk past or arrive, the shot
must begin without them — otherwise they simply stand there and the entrance
never happens.

ONE MAIN ACTION PER CLIP. Two characters both doing something, plus a camera
move, plus effects, plus a costume change is more than one shot can hold. When
the model runs out of capacity it drops character consistency first — the face
and hair drift. Give each clip a single job.

FREEZE THE SET DRESSING. Anything already drawn in the scene — a wall painting,
objects on a table — must be locked in words ("the painting on the wall does not
change, no new marks appear"), or the model keeps adding to it.
